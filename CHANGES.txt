After finishing my game, looking back at my initial one pager for the game design, I think I stuck really close to my original design. I wanted it to be a top down maze game which it is. I was able to keep the 2D spot light in a small 360 around the player which grows bigger when picking up a potion scattered around the maze. One thing I didn't end up doing was creating different monsters that could attack the player in the levels. If I had a lot more time I would have but I would of wanted a way for the player to pick up weapons and fight them but wasn't able to. I initially wanted to make secret traps, potentially hidden so the player would have to fall and die to know its there and to remember where it is after respawning to not make the same mistake again but I settled for lava that the player can see. Ultimately after playing the last two levels of my game, I actually was getting lost and it was taking me a lot longer than I expected to complete the them which made me think if someone were playing it for the first time, they wouldn't want to get too frustrated from being lost and then have to fight mobs and watch out for invisible traps as well. I stuck with using doors to get to the next level and finding a key hidden somewhere else in the maze to unlock it. I did make the levels progressively harder as I planned, but with the bigger levels I thought it would be better to have multiple keys to find and give the player more to pick up along the way instead of just having one key in a bigger maze. Overall, I was able to find the arts and assets I had in mind on the Unity store website for free which was nice and fit the pixilated dungeon style I was going for. I had to change the character to be a small knight instead of prisoner as I couldn't find an asset that I liked for prisoners, the key assets I found were great as well as the floors and walls. I changed the torch power up from being a diamond/gem to a potion. The game flow is pretty identical to how I imagined it and described it would in the beginning.
